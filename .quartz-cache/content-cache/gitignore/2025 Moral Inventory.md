## Chronological
### Elementary
I grew up with a lot of feelings of inadequacy. I often compared myself to my brother and sister and felt I didn't measure up to them. My parents would tell me "Why can't you be like your sister or brother?" 

Looking back, I now realize that ADHD played a large role in my feelings of inadequacy. I struggled at things that just came easier to others. I constantly forgot details, forgot homework, came late to things etc. I was constantly told "Daniel, you're so bright. You have so much potential, but you're just not responsible." 

Perhaps my first encounter with pornography was when I was in 3rd or 4th grade. There was a book in our house that had full frontal nudity. I was hooked on it. 
### Middle School

In middle school I was diagnosed with ADHD. I gave myself a stigma for this. I thought to myself, "Great, I'm one of **those** kids." whatever that meant. I incorrectly thought that ADHD was something I had to "figure out" and "get over". I thought one day I would conquer it and then I could be "normal". 

During this period of time also had intense recurring anxiety. Back then I didn't know that it was anxiety. I didn't have the self-awareness or the mental health education to realize what it was. Thankfully this was not on a daily basis, but when it occurred, it interfered with my life greatly. 

At this period, I had friends introduce me to pornography. I became deeply fixated on it. It was exhilarating and I foolishly tried to use it as a way to “learn” about sex. Really it just taught me how to use and be used by others. 
### High School

At the beginning of High School I reached crisis. 

I’m thankful for this period of crisis because it is what ultimately led me to finally choose to submit to Christ. 

While the Holy Spirit, reformed me from my impulsive lying, swearing, and anger, unfortunately porn proved to be more persistent in my life. 

In my Sophomore year, my grades finally started to stabilize. They weren't great, but they weren't constantly in crisis mode. During this time, my dad was encouraged that my grades had improved and my ADHD symptoms appeared to have gotten better. He asked me if maybe I had "grown out of it" and maybe I didn't need ADHD medication anymore. I wanted to be normal, I wanted to be past this, I wanted his approval, I wanted to believe that maybe I never had ADHD in the first place. So I agreed and we both decided to halt my medication. 

Looking back I now know that my symptoms were decreasing in part **because** my treatment was working. It did not mean that I had no ADHD. Thankfully, I had also learned valuable life skills which enabled me to better copy with my ADHD. They decreased my crises but they did not change my underlying brain chemistry, nor remove my ADHD.
