> A jack of all trades is a master of none,
> But often times better than a master of one. 

Most people know the first line of this phrase. But few people know the second line. This phrase teaches us that there is a [[trade-offs|trade-off]]. 

## Master of One
There is value to gaining [[mastery]] over a specific skill or domain. This takes time, effort, and discipline. There are no shortcuts.

## Master of None
But there is also value to having a wide, diverse set of skills and influences. 

### Case Studies
- A famous Looney Tunes director had his staff read a wide variety of books in order to gain a broad set of influences and inspirations.
