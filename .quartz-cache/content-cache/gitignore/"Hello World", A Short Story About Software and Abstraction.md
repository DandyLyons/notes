In the beginning there were computers: intelligent individuals who studied and even discovered math, science and logic. They came in many shapes and sizes across generations, sometimes wearing togas, sometimes spectacles. They accomplished great things: designing the dimensions of the pyramids, cracking complex ciphers, and calculating the trajectories of cannonballs. But as they increased in their ability, they also increased in the problems they must solve. **So they created new tools to help them compute.** Writing tablets, and abacuses, and slide rulers. These tools helped them for some time, **but eventually the problems got harder and more complex**, and even the best and brightest computers across the world could not keep up. 

Until, someone said "Why is my tool merely **helping** me to compute? Why can't it do the computation for me? Why can't we have a mechanical computer?" **So they created mechanical computers to compute for them.** Mechanical calculators, cash registers, and tabulating machines. These mechanical computers helped them for some time. And they improved to. Eventually mechanical gears became vacuum tubes became integrated circuits. **But eventually the problems got harder and more complex**, and even the best and brightest computer operators across the world could not keep up. Some looked back longingly at the past. "I miss my slide ruler." they said. "It was so much simpler without these computing machines."

Until, some said "Why is it so gosh darn hard to operate a computer? I can talk to a person with language and give them instructions. Why can't I communicate to a computer using language?" So they created programming languages to simplify the process of operating computers. These programming languages made computers so easy to use! Now it was just like talking to a person![^2] So they're productivity exploded! But eventually they realized, I put all this hard work into programming, and now my program only runs on this one computer. No other computer in the world can run my program. So they rewrote their programs again and again for each computer that it needed to run on. **But eventually the problems got harder and more complex.** There were too many programs that needed to run on too many different computers. 

---
What you have just read is a creative retelling of the history of computer science. It's not meant to be a history book. Rather it's meant to demonstrate an important recurring pattern:

1. Throughout history, humans have always encountered complex problems and solved them on their own. 
2. But over time, these solutions were costly[^1] due to the great compute required to overcome the great complexity.
3. So some humans created novel ways to automate this computation using hardware, software, frameworks or some other abstraction. 
4. The new abstraction simplified the workflow, thus dramatically increasing productivity. 
5. But the new abstraction made things more complex. (Back to step 1)
