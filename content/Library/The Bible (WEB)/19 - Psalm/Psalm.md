# Psalm

[[Ps-01|Start Reading →]]

## Structure 

| Book 1      | [Psalms 1–41](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+1%E2%80%9341/ "Psalms 1–41")          | [Psalms 1–2](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+1%E2%80%932/ "Psalms 1–2") provide an introduction to the Psalms as a whole. Except for [Psalms 10 and 33](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+10%3B+Psalm+33/ "Psalm 10; Psalm 33"), the remaining psalms of Book 1 are psalms of David. Most of them are prayers of distress. Others are statements of confidence in the God who alone can save (e.g., 9; 11; 16; 18), striking the note that concludes the book ([40–41](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+40%E2%80%9341/ "Psalms 40–41")). Reflections on ethics and worship are found in [Psalms 1; 14–15; 19; 24; and 26](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+1%3B+Psalms+14%E2%80%9315%3B+Psalm+19%3B+Psalm+24%3B+Psalm+26/ "Psalm 1; Psalms 14–15; Psalm 19; Psalm 24; Psalm 26").                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Book 2      | [Psalms 42–72](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+42%E2%80%9372/ "Psalms 42–72")       | Book 2 introduces the first group of psalms by the “sons of Korah” ([42; 44–49; 50](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+42%3B+Psalms+44%E2%80%9349%3B+Psalm+50/ "Psalm 42; Psalms 44–49; Psalm 50")). There are also more psalms of David ([51–65; 68–69](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+51%E2%80%9365%3B+Psalms+68%E2%80%9369/ "Psalms 51–65; Psalms 68–69")), including most of the “historical” psalms ([51–52; 54; 56–57; 59–60; 63](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+51%E2%80%9352%3B+Psalm+54%3B+Psalms+56%E2%80%9357%3B+Psalms+59%E2%80%9360%3B+Psalm+63/ "Psalms 51–52; Psalm 54; Psalms 56–57; Psalms 59–60; Psalm 63")). Once again, lament and distress dominate these prayers, which now also include a communal voice (e.g., 44; compare 67; 68). The lone psalm attributed to Solomon concludes Book 2 with a look at God’s ideal for Israel’s kings—ultimately pointing to Christ as the final great King of God’s people.                                               |
| Book 3      | [Psalms 73–89](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+73%E2%80%9389/ "Psalms 73–89")       | The tone darkens further in Book 3. The opening [Psalm 73](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+73/ "Psalm 73") starkly questions the justice of God before seeing light in God’s presence. That light has almost escaped the psalmist in [Psalm 88](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+88/ "Psalm 88"), the bleakest of all psalms. Book 2 ended with the high point of royal aspirations; Book 3 concludes in [Psalm 89](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+89/ "Psalm 89") with these expectations badly threatened. Sharp rays of hope occasionally pierce the darkness (e.g., 75; 85; 87). The brief third book contains most of the psalms of Asaph ([73–83](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+73%E2%80%9383/ "Psalms 73–83")), as well as another set of Korah psalms ([84–85; 87–88](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+84%E2%80%9385%3B+Psalms+87%E2%80%9388/ "Psalms 84–85; Psalms 87–88")).                                                                                                          |
| Book 4      | [Psalms 90–106](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+90%E2%80%93106/ "Psalms 90–106")    | [Psalm 90](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+90/ "Psalm 90") opens the fourth book of the psalms. It may be seen as the first response to the problems raised by Book 3. [Psalm 90](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+90/ "Psalm 90"), attributed to Moses, reminds the worshiper that God was active on Israel’s behalf long before David. This theme is taken up in [Psalms 103–106](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+103%E2%80%93106/ "Psalms 103–106"), which summarize God’s dealings with his people before any kings reigned. In between there is a group of psalms ([93–100](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+93%E2%80%93100/ "Psalms 93–100")) characterized by the refrain “The LORD reigns.” This truth refutes the doubts of [Psalm 89](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+89/ "Psalm 89").                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Book 5      | [Psalms 107–150](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+107%E2%80%93150/ "Psalms 107–150") | The structure of Book 5 reflects the closing petition of Book 4 in [106:47](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+106%3A47/ "Psalm 106:47"). It declares that God does answer prayer ([107](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+107/ "Psalm 107")) and concludes with five Hallelujah psalms ([146–150](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+146%E2%80%93150/ "Psalms 146–150")). In between there are several psalms affirming the validity of the promises to David ([110; 132; 144](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+110%3B+Psalm+132%3B+Psalm+144/ "Psalm 110; Psalm 132; Psalm 144")), two collections of Davidic psalms ([108–110; 138–145](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+108%E2%80%93110%3B+Psalms+138%E2%80%93145/ "Psalms 108–110; Psalms 138–145")); the longest psalm, celebrating the value of God’s law ([119](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+119/ "Psalm 119")); and 15 psalms of ascent for use by pilgrims to Jerusalem ([120–134](https://www.esv.org/Psalms+120%E2%80%93134/ "Psalms 120–134")). |
